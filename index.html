About Khorne

Khorne is one of the four major Chaos Gods, a vast power of the Warp bound to violence, rage, bloodshed and martial prowess. He is no patron of orderly war: his hunger for blood and skulls is never satisfied, and it turns his followers against allies, masters and one another.

Why Khorne is so powerful

Rage and bloodshed run through mortal life, and every war feeds the currents Khorne is bound to. He commands vast daemon hosts (Bloodletters, Bloodthirsters), mortal armies such as the World Eaters, and champions who offer skulls for his favour. His strength is raw, direct violence, backed by endless tribute.

That power has costs: his hunger can never be satisfied, his followers turn on one another, and he despises sorcery. Official lore does not rank the Chaos Gods, so he is neither strongest nor weakest by decree.

Khorne, the Blood God

Khorne is a Chaos God: a vast, sentient power of the Warp shaped by rage, hatred, bloodshed and the thrill of combat. Titles include the Blood God, Lord of Skulls and Bloodfather. He is shown enthroned on the Skull Throne amid piled trophies, and his seat in the Warp is the Brass Citadel (descriptions vary between publications).

His creed prizes strength, martial skill and skulls offered in tribute, and holds sorcery and cowardice in contempt. Yet his worship destroys restraint and stable loyalty. “Khorne cares not from whence the blood flows” sums it up: blood of enemy, ally or follower serves the same cycle. Eight is his sacred number, a recurring motif in daemonic organisation and symbolism.

The four Chaos Gods and the Great Game

Khorne: blood, war, rage, violence. Tzeentch: change, magic, ambition, schemes. Nurgle: decay, disease, endurance, despair. Slaanesh: excess, obsession, sensation, perfection.

The four are rivals, and their struggle for influence in the Warp is the Great Game, fought through daemons, cults, champions and bargains. Khorne and Tzeentch are the sharpest contrast, direct force against sorcery and plans. Official lore does not fix a permanent ranking of the gods, so this archive does not give one.

The Blood Legions: Khorne’s daemons

Bloodletters: horned, hellblade-wielding lesser daemons, the rank and file of the Blood Legions. Bloodthirsters: winged Greater Daemons, supreme commanders and champions. Flesh Hounds: swift daemon beasts that hunt prey, collared against hostile magic. Juggernauts and Bloodcrushers: daemon-machine beasts, ridden by Bloodletters as shock cavalry. Skull Cannons: daemonic war machines that give the legions ranged power.

Wrathmongers and Skullreapers are Age of Sigmar units, not 40,000 ones. Hierarchy and numbers vary between publications, and Bloodletters (daemons) are not Khorne Berzerkers (mortal Space Marines).

Named figures

Angron: Daemon Primarch of the World Eaters, once a gladiator slave on Nuceria. Khârn the Betrayer: a mortal World Eaters champion (see Khârn: Eater of Worlds). Ka’Bandha: Bloodthirster whose clashes with Sanguinius include Signus Prime. Skarbrand: a famous Bloodthirster tied to catastrophic rage and exile; check campaign sources for the details. Karanak: daemon hound of vengeance (Age of Sigmar material). Valkia the Bloody: Khorne champion of Warhammer Fantasy and Age of Sigmar, not a 40,000 figure.

Categories matter: Greater Daemons (Bloodthirsters), a Daemon Primarch (Angron) and mortal champions (Khârn) are distinct kinds of being.

Weapons and artefacts

The Skull Throne and Brass Citadel symbolise accumulated slaughter and his dominion. Hellblades arm Bloodletters; chainaxes are the World Eaters’ signature weapon. The Butcher’s Nails are pain-inducing implants that reward aggression and shaped Angron and his Legion.

Axes are Khorne’s dominant iconography, but this archive does not assert a single named “Axe of Khorne” artefact. Verify against an official source before attributing one.

World Eaters, Berzerkers and other followers

The World Eaters are the XII Legion, once the War Hounds, renamed after Angron joined them. In the 41st Millennium they are a Traitor Legion devoted to Khorne, known for close-combat assaults. Khorne Berzerkers are their shock troops, mortal Chaos Space Marines. Eightbound are World Eaters possessed by several daemons. Mortal cultists, murder cults and warbands also serve him, often fragmenting into rivalries over trophies and favour (the Path to Glory).

History and chronology (40,000 setting)

Nuceria: Angron is enslaved and fitted with the Butcher’s Nails. Reunion: the Emperor’s son joins the War Hounds, who become the World Eaters. Horus Heresy: Angron and the Legion side with Horus; Khorne’s daemons appear, including Ka’Bandha at Signus Prime. Siege of Terra: the World Eaters join the assault, one of several Chaos forces with competing agendas. After the Heresy: the Legion fragments and Khârn rises. Era Indomitus: Angron returns in major campaigns.

Age of Sigmar and The Old World events are separate settings and are not part of this timeline.

Enemies and rivals

The Imperium of Man and the Emperor oppose Khornate forces, though the Emperor’s power is debated in-universe and no official duel with Khorne is established. Among the gods: Tzeentch (force against sorcery), Slaanesh (violence against sensation) and Nurgle (rage against decay). His followers also fight each other. The Blood Angels’ Red Thirst and Black Rage do not make them servants of Khorne.

The Warp and Chaos

The Warp (Immaterium) is a psychic dimension of emotion and thought, used for faster-than-light travel and dangerous to enter. Chaos Gods are Warp powers bound to recurring emotional currents; mortal feeling can feed them and their influence can inflame mortals. Where the barrier thins, in Warp storms or rituals, daemons manifest or possess hosts and corruption spreads. In-universe explanations may be biased or wrong, so treat them with care.

Archivist’s guide: adding posts

Edit only Post.txt. One record per line, fields separated by $:

TYPE$TITLE$DESCRIPTION$URL1$URL2$URL3

Types: TEXT, PHOTO, PHOTOS, VIDEO, VIDEOS, PHOTO_VIDEO, MIXED, YOUTUBE, AUDIO. Title, description and URLs are optional. Lines starting with # and blank lines are ignored. Write a literal dollar sign as \$ and a line break as \n. Normal GitHub file URLs work for media. A line with no TYPE (TITLE$DESCRIPTION$URL…) is also accepted and its type is detected. Identical lines count as one record.

TEXT$The Skull Throne$Skulls for the Skull Throne.
PHOTOS$Throne$Two views$https://github.com/me/repo/blob/main/a.jpg$https://github.com/me/repo/blob/main/b.png
YOUTUBE$Chronicle$A video$https://youtu.be/XXXXXXXXXXX
AUDIO$War horn$Clip$https://example.com/horn.mp3

Change POST_SOURCE_URL at the top of script.js to use another file.
